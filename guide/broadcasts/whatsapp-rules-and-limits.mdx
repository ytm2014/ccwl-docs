WhatsApp is the strictest channel for broadcasts. WhatsApp itself decides what a business may send and to whom, and the broadcast composer applies those rules before anything goes out. Knowing them saves you from failed sends and from a number that loses its sending reputation.

## The 24-hour window

You can send a free-form message on WhatsApp only within 24 hours of the customer's last message to you. Outside that window, the only thing you may send is a **message template** that WhatsApp (Meta) has approved.

A broadcast goes to many people at once, and most of them won't have written in the last 24 hours. That is why a WhatsApp broadcast always sends an approved template: the composer has no free-text box on WhatsApp, and only approved templates are listed. See [Message templates](/guide/broadcasts/message-templates).

## Template categories matter

Each template has a category, chosen when it is created and checked by Meta:

| Category | Used for | How broadcasts treat it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Marketing** | Promotions, offers, announcements | Counts as a promotion. Only reaches contacts with a recorded WhatsApp opt-in, and never contacts who told WhatsApp to stop marketing messages. |
| **Utility** | Order, booking and account updates | Counts as a service message. Reaches contacts without a recorded opt-in, and contacts who opted out of promotions only. |
| **Authentication** | One-time codes | Counts as a service message. |

Meta reviews every template before it can be sent, so choose the category that matches what the message really is.

## Opt-in and opt-out

Before a marketing template can reach someone, your workspace must have a record that they agreed to receive WhatsApp messages from you. How a contact gets one, and how an admin can include contacts without it, is explained in [Choose a broadcast audience](/guide/broadcasts/audiences#contacts-without-a-whatsapp-opt-in).

People can also say no:

- A contact who **opted out**, is **blocked** or carries the **Unsubscribe** tag is never messaged by a broadcast.
- A contact who asked WhatsApp to stop marketing messages from your number is left out of every marketing template. Nobody in your workspace can override that.
- If your workspace lets contacts opt out of promotions only, they still receive utility templates.

## The number's messaging limit

WhatsApp gives every business number a **messaging limit**: how many different people it may message with templates in a rolling 24 hours.

The broadcast composer reads this limit and plans around it:

- In the Audience step and the review, if the audience is bigger than what the number has left, you see a note such as "This number's WhatsApp messaging limit lets 1,000 of 2,400 go out now. The rest are sent automatically over the next 2 days."
- The rest of the audience is held and released in daily waves. The broadcast stays **Sending** until everyone is handled, and its report shows how many are still waiting for the limit.
- The count is deliberately careful: every template the number sent in the last 24 hours counts, including from flows and the inbox, and so do messages that other broadcasts on the number have queued.
- If WhatsApp hasn't reported the number's limit, the composer says it could not be checked. The broadcast is then sent in full, and WhatsApp may refuse recipients over the limit.

The limit applies to WhatsApp templates only. Free-form replies inside the 24-hour window, and other channels, are not affected.

## Sending speed

Even within the limit, messages don't all leave at once. Recipients are released in batches each minute, and each number sends a limited number of messages per second. A large broadcast can take a while to finish; nothing is lost while it waits.

## Meta can still hold messages back

Some messages are accepted and then not delivered for reasons on WhatsApp's side. The report's **Failures and skips** names them, for example:

- **Meta held this back to protect engagement**: WhatsApp limits how many marketing messages one person receives from businesses.
- **Meta held this back as part of a marketing message experiment.**
- **Provider paused or disabled the template**: WhatsApp stopped the template. Pick another one.
- **The WhatsApp Business account has a payment problem** or **is locked**: fix it in WhatsApp Manager.

## Test before you send

**Send test message** in the composer sends the template to your own WhatsApp number (the one on your profile) so you can check the variables and buttons. See [Create a broadcast](/guide/broadcasts/create-broadcast#send-a-test-message-whatsapp).

## Common questions

**Why did fewer people receive my broadcast than the segment count?**
The count shows eligible contacts when you build the broadcast. At send time, contacts without an opt-in (for marketing templates), people who opted out since, and people WhatsApp itself held back are skipped. The report lists each reason.

**Why is my broadcast still Sending the next day?**
Part of the audience is waiting for the number's messaging limit. It finishes automatically.

**Can I send a WhatsApp broadcast without a template?**
No. Only approved templates can be broadcast on WhatsApp.

Every published post has its own analytics, collected from each account it went out on. Use them to see which version and which network did best, and how the post compares with the account's usual results.

## Open a post's analytics

1. Open a published post, from the [posts list](/guide/publishing/posts) or the [calendar](/guide/publishing/calendar).
2. Click the **Analytics** tab in the composer's side panel.

While figures are loading you see **Loading...**. If none have been collected yet, the tab says **No performance data yet for this post.**

## Overview

The **Overview** sums the post up "Across ... accounts":

| Figure | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Views** | How many times the post was seen. |
| **Likes** | Likes and reactions. |
| **Comments** | Comments and replies. |
| **Shares** | Shares, reposts and similar. |
| **Engagement rate** | The post's engagement rate across accounts. |
| **Peak day** | The day with the most interactions, with the number. |

Small trend lines appear next to figures once there are at least two data points.

Below the overview:

- **Engagement funnel**: from **Views** to **Interactions** to **Shares**.
- **Engagement breakdown**: how the interactions split between likes, comments, shares and so on.

## Top performing account

When a post went to several accounts, **Top performing account** highlights the one that did best, with its engagement rate and its views, likes, comments and shares.

## Per account

Each account then has its own panel:

- Its engagement rate.
- **... x vs account avg**: "How this post compares to the average performance of all posts on this account." Above 1 means the post did better than usual.
- The network's figures for the post, with trend lines.
- A breakdown of interactions.

If a network provides nothing for the post, its panel says **No analytics data available for this account.**

## Act on the results

- Reuse a strong post: duplicate it from the posts list, or use **Post it again** in **Reuse a winner** on [Home](/guide/publishing/home).
- Compare versions: if one network's version did much better, try its wording on the others.
- Look at the time it went out and the **Best times to post** when you schedule the next one.
